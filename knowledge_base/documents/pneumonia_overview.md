## 1. Definition

Pneumonia is an infection of the lungs. It affects the small air sacs in the lungs, known as alveoli. During pneumonia, the alveoli can become filled with fluid or inflammatory material, which can interfere with normal oxygen exchange.

Pneumonia can range from mild illness to severe illness.

---

## 2. Common Causes

Pneumonia can be caused by different types of infectious organisms.

Common causes include:

- Bacteria
- Viruses
- Fungi
- Less commonly, parasites

The organisms responsible for pneumonia can vary depending on the setting and the individual.

Community-acquired pneumonia refers to pneumonia acquired outside a healthcare setting.

Healthcare-associated forms of pneumonia can occur during or following healthcare exposure.

Ventilator-associated pneumonia can occur in people receiving mechanical ventilation.

---

## 3. Common Symptoms

Symptoms can vary depending on the cause and severity.

Common symptoms may include:

- Cough
- Fever
- Chills
- Shortness of breath
- Fatigue
- Chest pain, particularly with breathing or coughing
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Confusion or altered mental status, particularly in some older adults

The presence of one or more symptoms does not by itself establish a diagnosis of pneumonia.

---

## 4. Risk Factors

Risk of pneumonia can be influenced by several factors.

Examples include:

- Age
- Certain underlying health conditions
- Weakened immune systems
- Certain behaviors
- Environmental exposures

Risk factors vary between individuals and different types of pneumonia.

---

## 5. Prevention

Measures that can reduce the risk of pneumonia include:

- Appropriate vaccination
- Good hand hygiene
- Reducing exposure to tobacco smoke
- Reducing indoor air pollution
- Maintaining adequate nutrition
- Following appropriate infection-prevention practices

Vaccination can help prevent some infections that can lead to pneumonia.

---

## 6. Diagnosis

Pneumonia diagnosis requires clinical assessment.

Healthcare professionals may consider:

- Symptoms
- Medical history
- Physical examination
- Oxygenation status
- Laboratory testing when appropriate
- Imaging when appropriate

A chest X-ray may provide useful clinical information, but an image alone should not be treated as a complete substitute for clinical evaluation.

---

## 7. Chest X-Ray Context

Chest X-rays can be used as part of the evaluation of suspected pneumonia.

Radiographic findings can vary depending on:

- The underlying cause
- Disease severity
- Patient characteristics
- Timing of imaging
- Image quality
- Positioning

Radiographic appearance alone should therefore not be interpreted as a definitive diagnosis without appropriate clinical context.

---

## 8. Treatment Context

Treatment depends on the underlying cause, severity, patient characteristics, and clinical setting.

Bacterial pneumonia may require antibiotics, while antibiotics do not treat viral infections.

Treatment decisions should be made by qualified healthcare professionals.

This knowledge base intentionally does not provide patient-specific treatment recommendations or medication dosing.

## 10. AI Safety and Limitations

The RAG system should not:

- Diagnose a real patient
- Recommend medication for a real patient
- Generate a real person's medical record
- Claim that a generated X-ray is clinically authentic
- Claim that synthetic data is automatically HIPAA compliant
- Present generated information as verified clinical advice

The system should instead provide synthetic examples for software development, testing, research, and educational experimentation.
